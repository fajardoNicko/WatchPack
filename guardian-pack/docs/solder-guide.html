WatchPack solder guide

You'll make five joints on the board and two small parts off it: the SOS button with its two wires, and the buzzer driver. Every pin here comes from the wiring page we checked against LilyGO's V1.4 schematic and your board photos. Tick each step as you go; your ticks stay on this phone.

Use any jumper colours you have. These names just keep the three buzzer wires apart. Positions are always counted 1 → 16 from the ESP32 end. The PY edge has the ON/OFF switch; the PX edge has the USB-C port.

Before you heat the iron

Part 1 · off the board

SOS button wires

Normally closed means the button conducts until you press it. Wire either lug to either pin; it doesn't matter which.

Part 2 · off the board

Buzzer driver

How it works: IO18 goes HIGH → the transistor switches on → the buzzer's − side connects to ground → beep. The buzzer's current comes from the battery, not the ESP32 pin, and the 1 kΩ keeps IO18 safe.

Part 3 · on the board

Five header pins

Each pin goes in from the back (battery side), where the labels are, and gets soldered on the front. Flipping the board swaps left and right, so find the edges by the switch and the USB-C port, never by "left" or "right".

Bold labels are printed and readable on your board. Grey italic ones are hidden under the holder or the GPS board. Red labels are neighbours you must keep solder away from.

The two small squares by the ESP32 are the RST and IO0 buttons, on the PX edge. On the front you'll see only five pin tails poking through. Those are your five joints. The red rings are the holes next to them; a solder bridge onto one of those is the mistake to avoid.

Watch these neighbours. A blob of solder that reaches the next hole causes:
  • PY2 next to PY1 → 3.3 V shorted to GND.
  • PY16 next to PY15 (IO12, power enable) → battery voltage straight onto an ESP32 pin.
  • PX9 next to PX8 (GPS wake) and PX10 (modem reset) → GPS or modem stops working.
  • PX15 next to PX16 (USB 5 V) and PX14 (RST) → 5 V short, or a board stuck in reset.
