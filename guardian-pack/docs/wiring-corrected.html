What changed from the image

Charger was backwards. The cell ran straight to the ESP32 while the TP4056 hung off to the side with power flowing into its IN pins. Now: USB → IN, cell → B, load → OUT.

3.7 V cell fed VIN. The DevKit's AMS1117 regulator needs ≥5 V; a sagging cell collapses the 3.3 V rail and the board resets mid-transmit. VIN now comes off the boosted 5 V rail.

UART was off by one pin. A GPIO was driving the modem's GND pin and the modem's RXD sat on ground. Now GND↔GND, GPIO17→RXD, GPIO16←TXD.

PWRKEY was floating. Most SIM7600/A7670 boards never power on without it. Wired to GPIO4.

Antennas went to header pins. NET, STA and GPS are status outputs. Antennas belong on the u.FL sockets.

Cap sat out on the rail. Moved to the modem's own VCC/GND pins, where the current spike actually happens.

Before you power it on

Verify the modem's input voltage first. A bare SIM7600E/A7670 module wants 3.4–4.2 V and 5 V will destroy it. Only breakouts with an onboard regulator accept 5 V. If yours is a bare module, feed it from the cell through an MP1584 buck set to 3.8 V instead of the MT3608, and take the ESP32's 5 V from a separate boost. Confirm against your board's silkscreen and datasheet when it arrives.
  • Set the MT3608 output with a multimeter before connecting any load — they ship at arbitrary voltages and the pot has many turns.
  • Check continuity on the common ground rail before applying power.
  • SIM card: PIN lock disabled, data plan loaded, and already activated by sending one SMS from a phone.
  • Keep antennas away from the cell and any metal. First GPS fix outdoors takes 1–2 minutes — that is normal, not a fault.
  • Bring the subsystems up in the order set out in docs/03-firmware.md: buzzer, GPS, SMS, HTTP POST, buzz command, then integrate.
